ALGORITHM 01

SCAN Disk Scheduling

Also known as the elevator algorithm.

01 / 02
HOW IT WORKS

A sweep, then a reversal

SCAN moves the disk head in the selected direction and services requests encountered along the way. It continues to the disk boundary, then reverses and services requests in the opposite direction.

  1. 1Start at the current head position.
  2. 2Service requests in the chosen direction.
  3. 3Reach the disk boundary, then reverse.
  4. 4Service remaining requests on the return sweep.
QUICK REVIEW

Strengths & trade-offs

+

Advantages
More orderly service than purely arrival-order scheduling; limits indefinite postponement compared with some policies.

−

Disadvantages
Requests just behind the head may wait for another sweep; travelling to a boundary can add movement even when no request is there.

Actual performance depends on request distribution, head position and disk configuration.

ALGORITHM 02

C-SCAN Disk Scheduling

Circular SCAN: requests are serviced in one direction.

02 / 02
HOW IT WORKS

A circular service pattern

C-SCAN services requests only while moving in one direction. After reaching the disk boundary, the head returns to the opposite boundary without servicing requests on the return, then resumes the same direction.

  1. 1Start at the current head position.
  2. 2Service requests in the chosen direction.
  3. 3Reach the boundary, then wrap to the opposite end.
  4. 4Continue servicing in the original direction.
QUICK REVIEW

Strengths & trade-offs

+

Advantages
Services requests in one direction, which can provide more uniform waiting-time behaviour across cylinders.

−

Disadvantages
The wrap-around adds head travel without servicing requests; it may produce more movement than SCAN for some queues.

C-SCAN is not always faster. Compare results for the actual request queue.

WHICH ONE IS BETTER — AND WHY?

Choose SCAN when…

Lower movement matters for this workload. It can avoid C-SCAN’s end-to-start wrap cost and may be more efficient for some request distributions. It services requests on both sweeps, but waiting time depends on where a request enters the sweep.

External study: Yusuf & Istiono (2020) report that SCAN can be faster for datasets with small standard deviation and variance.

Choose C-SCAN when…

More uniform waiting time is the priority. It services requests in one direction and returns without servicing requests, giving requests a more consistent directional service pattern. The wrap-around can increase total movement.

External study: Yusuf & Istiono (2020) report more uniform waiting times for C-SCAN; the supplied research PDF also notes C-SCAN’s fairness trade-off.

There is no universal winner. Use the live results above for your input queue, then consider both head movement and fairness. Research findings depend on the tested request sets and assumptions.

i

Calculation convention: the simulator counts travel to the relevant disk boundary and C-SCAN wrap-around as head movement. Average movement per request is total head movement divided by the number of requests serviced. These are cylinder-distance measures, not milliseconds.

01 2021 · REVIEW Analysis of Traditional Hard Disk Scheduling Algorithms: A Review Mustafa Ali Bamboat and co-authors · QRJ · 19(1), 51–58 +

Authors: Mustafa Ali Bamboat, Mansoor Ahmed Khuhro, Kamlesh Kumar, Imtiaz Ali Halepoto, Naadiya Mirbahar, Ghulam Sarfaraz Khan

Year & source: 2021; Quaid-e-Awam University Research Journal of Engineering, Science and Technology, Vol. 19, Issue 1, pp. 51–58.

Problem addressed

Traditional disk-scheduling algorithms exhibit major differences in seek latency, queue throughput, and service fairness under varying request loads, creating the need for a systematic comparative assessment.

Proposed approach

Compares FCFS, SSTF, SCAN, C-SCAN, LOOK, C-LOOK, OTHDSA (Optimized Traditional Hard Disk Scheduling Algorithm), and Zone-Based Disk Scheduling across multiple experimental I/O request datasets.

Key findings

OTHDSA delivered the best performance in the reported tests, with seek times of 175 and 139 units across two test runs. The paper also demonstrates that directional sweeping algorithms like SCAN can benefit from dynamic grouping of track requests.

Limitations: Focuses strictly on traditional moving-head disk setups, and performance varies depending on request distribution.

Relevance to our project: Supplies reference benchmarking datasets to test and validate the baseline SCAN and C-SCAN simulation implementations.

02 2021 · COMPARATIVE STUDY A Comparative Analysis of Disk Scheduling Algorithms Bishwo Prakash Pokharel · IJRASB · 8(2), 184–195 +

Author: Bishwo Prakash Pokharel

Year & source: 2021; International Journal for Research in Applied Sciences and Biotechnology, Vol. 8, Issue 2, pp. 184–195.

Problem addressed

Minimizing total head movement and arm turnaround delay across changing I/O queues.

Proposed approach

Examines head traversal distances and seek-time metrics across six standard disk-scheduling policies under diverse arrival orderings.

Key findings

The supplied project notes report that C-LOOK yields the lowest seek overheads by eliminating boundary scans. They also note that while C-SCAN has higher total movement than LOOK variants, it maintains better waiting-time consistency.

Limitations: Evaluation relies on simulated sequences and does not address modern solid-state architectures.

Relevance to our project: Helps establish comparison metrics between boundary-traversing algorithms (SCAN/C-SCAN) and edge-limited algorithms, supporting the comparative dashboard.

03 2022 · PROPOSED VARIATION Pre-Reset the R/W Head to Zero Track in SCAN and C-SCAN Disk Scheduling Algorithm Ahmed Mohamed Malallah Al-Suffar · IJSER · 1(1), 103–108 +

Author: Assist. Prof. Ahmed Mohamed Malallah Al-Suffar

Year & source: 2022; Al-Iraqia Journal for Scientific Engineering Research, Vol. 1, Issue 1, pp. 103–108.

Problem addressed

Traditional SCAN and C-SCAN algorithms can cause unnecessary head movement and non-uniform response times when sweeping to the disk edge without pending requests.

Proposed approach

Introduces a pre-reset mechanism that automatically moves the read/write head to track 0 after servicing current requests, standardizing the forward sweep.

Key findings

The supplied project material reports 183 seek units compared with 253 for SCAN and 188 for C-SCAN on the tested request set, with higher throughput and more predictable waiting times.

Limitations: Tested primarily on moving-head models under fixed request patterns.

Relevance to our project: A candidate approach for Stage 3 because its logic extends SCAN and C-SCAN and could be explored in the interactive simulator.

04 2024 · NEW ALGORITHM Proposing Disk Scheduling Algorithm to Enhance the Efficiency of Disk Performance G. Elnaas and M. Sullabi · IJEIT · 12(1), 111–119 +

Authors: G. Elnaas and M. Sullabi

Year & source: 2024; The International Journal of Engineering & Information Technology (IJEIT), Vol. 12, No. 1, pp. 111–119.

Problem addressed

Mechanical seek delays continue to bottleneck overall processing performance because of the speed gap between high-speed CPUs and mechanical storage.

Proposed approach

Proposes an algorithm that organizes requests and selects head paths using nearest-neighbouring tracks alongside boundary checks.

Key findings

In Sample I, the proposed method achieved 177 total head movements and 22.125 ms average seek time, compared with SCAN (236 movements / 29.5 ms) and C-SCAN (382 movements / 47.75 ms).

Limitations: Validated using three discrete sample queues.

Relevance to our project: Supplies direct benchmarks for our sample request queue (176, 79, 34, 60, 92, 11, 41, 114).

05 2024 · UNIVERSAL APPROACH Optimized Universal Disk Scheduling Algorithm Amar Ranjan Dash and co-authors · Journal of The Institution of Engineers (India): Series B, Springer +

Authors: Amar Ranjan Dash, Baishnobi Dash, Sandipta Kumar Sahu, Neha Kumari Sahu, Srinibash Panda

Year & source: 2024; Journal of The Institution of Engineers (India): Series B, Springer.

Problem addressed

Standard scheduling approaches consider only single-track seek time, overlooking rotational latency, platter-selection overhead, and multi-disk/RAID configurations.

Proposed approach

Implements a universal scheduling method designed to optimize composite access costs across varied disk geometries, partitions, and RAID systems.

Key findings

The approach aims to lower overall access overhead by managing multi-platter and multi-disk operations simultaneously.

Limitations: Focuses on multi-dimensional hardware parameters, making it more complex than single-surface SCAN/C-SCAN comparisons.

Relevance to our project: Broadens the scope beyond one-dimensional cylinder paths to enterprise disk arrays.
